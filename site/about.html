How it works, and where it stops working

The idea

Seismometers are built to record earthquakes, but between quakes they record people: traffic, trains, machinery and footsteps shake the ground by a few nanometres, mostly at 4–14 Hz. During the 2020 lockdowns, Lecocq et al. showed this "human noise" dropped worldwide. GroundTruth turns that discarded signal into a daily reading of each city's rhythm.

What happens every day

  1. A GitHub Action downloads the latest hourly noise spectra from EarthScope's free MUSTANG service (they arrive about 2 days late).
  2. Each local day becomes 25 numbers: the shape of its 24 hours (when the city was busy) and its overall level, scaled against that station's own normal days.
  3. Two random-forest models per station, trained on 1 Oct 2025 – 30 Sep 2026, guess the weekday and whether it was a weekend. Every day on the Today page is after that window, so the model has never seen it.
  4. A day is marked unusual if it is further from normal than 95% of the training year's normal days were (scored with those days' own weeks held out). So about 1 normal day in 20 is flagged by chance: one flag means little; a cluster means something.

Ask the ground: any day, 2019 to 2026

On the Ask page you pick a city and a day, and a model says which weekday it thinks that day was. Each of the 33 cities has its own random forest, trained on ordinary days from January 2019 to September 2026, with whole weeks held out: every answer you see comes from a model that never trained on that day's week. Public holidays, 21 December – 6 January, and a fixed lockdown window (1 March – 30 June 2020) are never used for training at all. The model sees only the day's 24-hour shape and how loud the day was against the 8 weeks before it; it is never told the date. Later local lockdowns (Melbourne's, for example) were not marked, so those days did enter training.

Sound

The site makes its own sound in your browser (a slow drone, a whoosh when the Earth moves, a pen while a line draws, a chime when an answer appears). There are no audio files, and nothing plays until you click. The Sound switch in the bar turns it off, and the site remembers. Every sound goes with something you can see, so you miss nothing with it off.

How good is it?

Tested with whole weeks held out on the training year: weekday vs weekend 98–100% in Brussels, Los Angeles, Melbourne, Dublin and Montreal; 72% at the UAE station; 65% at the remote control town. Each city on the Today page also shows its live track record: how the model has done on the new days since 1 Oct 2026. Full results: docs/04-results.md.

Limits — please read

  • No data means no data. If a station is down, the Today page says so. We never fill gaps.
  • Public holidays are national calendars only. Regional holidays (e.g. Quebec's) are not marked.
  • Montreal's training year misses most of Feb–May 2026, and its first live month is noisier than its training scores suggest.
  • The UAE station (II.UOSS, site name "Univ. of Sharjah") sits ~90 km east of Sharjah city per its official coordinates. The weekend labels follow the federal Saturday–Sunday rule.
  • "Unusual" says the ground was different, not why. Weather, construction or a sensor problem can all cause it.
  • Models are not retrained automatically; the version and training window are shown on the page.

Privacy

The data is the combined vibration of a whole neighbourhood, summarised per hour. It cannot identify, locate or follow any person, and we don't try to.
