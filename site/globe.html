Seven years of city life, from the ground

Pick any day since January 2019. Each dot is a public seismometer; its colour says whether the city shook less (blue) or more (red) than on the same weekday over the previous eight weeks. Try March 2020, then a Christmas.

How to read this

  • What a colour means. For each station and day we take the day's vibration level (4–14 Hz, the frequencies of traffic and footsteps) and compare it with the same weekday over the previous 8 weeks, ignoring public holidays. −30% means the ground shook 30% less than a usual Monday (or Tuesday…) there had recently.
  • Why "recent weeks" and not one fixed year. Seismometers change over the years: new sensors, building work next door. Over a few weeks that barely matters; over years it does (Sharjah's level jumps 1.5× for months at a time). The price: a long event such as a lockdown fades out after about 8 weeks, because it becomes the new "normal", and the reopening afterwards shows up red (louder than the quiet weeks before it). Brussels in 2020 is a good example: blue in March–April, red through the summer.
  • Faded dots are towns where the published study could barely read a weekly rhythm (below 80% weekday-vs-weekend accuracy), or with no published score. Their colours are mostly weather, waves and sensor noise.
  • Hollow dots: no usable data that day (station offline, or fewer than 3 normal reference days).
  • The dot is the seismometer, which is often on a campus or hill near the city, not its centre. Coordinates from the operators' station service. Where a sensor is more than 20 km from the city the 2020 paper named, the label says where it really is: the paper's "Sao Paulo" sensor is in Valinhos, near Campinas, and its "Sharjah" sensor is a mountain vault 91 km away, near Kalba. Distances from the GeoNames gazetteer.
  • Coverage. 33 stations from the published study that the EarthScope MUSTANG service still serves; Europe is thin because European stations are archived elsewhere. India has only two, both at its edges (Port Blair in the Andaman Islands, Shillong in Meghalaya): the 2020 data has no station on the Indian mainland. History runs 1 Jan 2019 – 30 Sep 2026; newer days are on the Today page.
  • Check. On the 6 cities where both exist, this data reproduces the published 2020 lockdown quieting within 0.7 percentage points (Result 14).
